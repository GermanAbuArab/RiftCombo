## 3. Things I tried that did NOT work — do not repeat them

- **Minimum PRINTED Might over `uses[]` as a fragility proxy.** Comes back flat (3.0/3.0/3.5) and says
  the gradient does not exist. **A token is never a `uses[]` row**, so it is blind to exactly the
  population the claim is about. Use rule 187.
- **"Does the entry argue about Might" as a proxy.** 6 of 10 fast against 7 of 10 slow — no signal,
  and it cannot tell an entry NAMING its vulnerability from one FIXING it.
- **A positional id extractor on a clock row.** The `[BANNED in constructed]` marker shifts the
  columns; it reported 4 where the truth is 11. **Intersect the row's tokens with the catalogue's id
  set.**
- **An object spread to merge a bucket with a clock row.** `{...clk.get(id)}` clobbered the bucket
  field `b` with the baseline `b`. Printed "5 / SLOWER" and looked like data.
- **Scope detection from a regex match window.** `Other friendly units have +1 Might **here**` puts the
  scoping word outside a 40- and a 60-character window. Bit me twice. **Read the card.**
- **Raising the exact-allocator guard to fit bigger closures.** 2e6 takes 50 seconds with `[Equip]`
  priced. 2e5 is 0.34s and one fallback.
- **A prose sweep for "entry names another entry but declares no `needs`".** 7 hits, all 7 legitimate
  (cross-references, anti-references, optional outlets, and one entry that CONTAINS the engine it
  points at). **Zero broken DAG links in 766 entries.**

## 4. The frame the whole lane converged on

**Three board states, not two.** Hold BOTH — the free curve wins on T5-T6 and no finisher is wanted.
Hold ONE — 1 a turn, 8 on T9, **the ordinary game and the board a finisher is for**. Hold NONE — a
Hold-gated line is off and only the ATTACK and CONQUER shapes live. Both instruments modelled two
until today, which is why "N of 80 pay later than doing nothing" read as an indictment of the
catalogue when it was a statement about the wrong board.

And the trade every one of the five plays landed on, measured over 624 entries: **you can be fast or
you can be above the Might-3 line the pool prices its removal at, and mostly not both.**

## 5. The specific next step I would take

**Model ORDERING in `deployTurn`.** It is the last known gap and I found it on my own fifth play:
the allocator asks only whether every cost is *payable* by turn N, so it will pay a Svellsongur's
`[Equip]` before the Svellsongur is on the board. `svellsongur-copy-hold` is T6 by the allocator and
**T7 by a hand walk that respects ordering**. That is a real constraint on every `[Equip]` line and on
anything whose cost depends on a card already being in play.

It is a bigger change than the cost parser was: the state would have to carry which cards are on the
board, not just which costs are paid. **Do not bolt it on** — the same reasoning that stopped me
approximating `[Equip]` applies. If it cannot be done exactly within the runtime budget, document the
gap in the header the way the INFINITE over-estimate already is.

Two smaller ones if you want something bounded first:
- **The `--engines` hole sweep.** The answer sweep's population is the finisher classes by design
  (#203) and `--engines` suppresses it rather than widening it. Whether the ENGINE class should get
  its own is an open decision nobody has made.
- **The INFINITE over-estimate.** Recorded in the header, deliberately unpatched: the clock prices
  every card against the rune curve, and an Energy engine pays for its own later cards. A fix needs a
  model of when a loop ignites.
