## 3. The findings, compressed

- **fury/chaos is the only legend pair with zero spanning finishers, and three measured mechanisms
  explain it** — Fury's two multipliers read conquer and hold effects while Chaos's two point cards
  trigger on a move and a combat win; all ten Fury/Chaos dual-domain printings sit in ENGINE entries
  only; and Tryndamere's gate and Draven's gate are opposite outcomes of the same damage step, with
  465.2.c giving the OPPONENT the choice of which of your bodies dies and 466.3.c giving the combat
  result only to *"units at this battlefield"*.
- **The whole credential class of the catalogue is two printed cards.** `--stalled`'s ATTACK bucket
  is eight rows, four of which score nothing; the four that score stand on `OGN-034 Tryndamere` and
  `SFD-148 Draven`, and a sweep says those are the only two point cards in the pool gated on the
  opponent's board. They are mono-fury and mono-chaos, so **the one identity that can run both is the
  one identity where they cannot be combined.**
- **469.2 says MAINTAINS and 469.1 says GAINS**, which decides what a Time Warp can multiply: a Hold
  repeats, a Conquer starves (348.2.a), a combat starves harder (323.9). Eleven of eleven entries
  obeyed it before it was written down. Now pinned as a structure.
- **The spanning axis is sound for finishers and 17% inflated for engines** (62→61 against 344→284
  when legend rows are excluded), and **one entry carries the whole confound in both directions**.
- **Seven cards read one board shape** — a battlefield with exactly two friendly units — and the
  catalogue had it pairwise, never as a shape.
- **MIXING POINT SOURCES IS DOMINATED, AND THE MECHANISM IS N × (1 + K).** Of 62 spanning finishers
  only **4** draw points from both halves; 32 take points from one and support from the other. Points
  from one source scale as N copies × (1 + K multipliers), so **a multiplier adds N and a second point
  card adds 1** — and the second can never be rescued by the first's multiplier, because 383.4.d.3
  makes a Hold Effect **triggered** and 377.1 makes an activated ability activated *by the presence of
  the colon*. The 32 spend the partner domain on a multiplier; the 4 spend it on a point card.
- **THE SPANNING QUESTION IS CLOSED BY ENUMERATION, NOT BY SURVEY.** Exactly two routes exist. Route
  two needs a Hold-gated and a combat-gated point card in one identity, and the pool prints only two
  combat-gated ones (`OGN-034` mono-fury, `SFD-148` mono-chaos) — so it is available only to fury or
  chaos, **and fury/chaos, which holds both, is the one pair where they cannot be combined.** Route one
  needs an Equipment point source, and the pool prints exactly one, `SFD-115 Trinity Force`, which is
  **Body** — so it is available to the five Body pairs, of which three were built, **body/fury is now
  built and takes both routes at once**, and body/mind is dominated by its own multiplier. **No
  sixteenth cell and no third route.**

## 4. What I would tell my successor first — and #200 is half finished, not finished

**THE ENTRY-SYNTHESIS VEIN IS FINISHED AND I WOULD NOT REOPEN IT.** The spanning question is closed by
**enumeration rather than by survey** (§3), which is a stronger kind of closed: there are exactly two
routes, each is gated on a card the pool prints exactly one or two of, and every one of the fifteen
pairs is accounted for. **The reopening condition is printed and should be checked against any new
set: a second Equipment point source, or a combat-gated point card outside fury and chaos.** Either
one puts the question back. Nothing else does.

**WHAT NOT TO SPEND TIME ON, which is the more useful half:**

1. **Do not re-run a "which pair is empty" census in any form.** For finishers §13–§14 close it; for
   ENGINEs §11 shows it is *a different question* — 69% of ENGINEs are two-card entries, so "spans two
   domains" there means only "these two cards differ in domain", and the class already has a
   purpose-built gap instrument in the synergy matrix.
2. **Do not treat the multiplier × point-card grid as open.** The manager built it (60 cells, 18
   covered) and §14's mechanism explains why it collapses rather than merely that it does: **a
   multiplier multiplies the KIND of effect it names**, so the three point cards that gain by card
   text can be reached by nothing, and a battlefield point card cannot share a board with a
   battlefield multiplier under 485.4.a with 103.4.c.
3. **Do not port the spanning axis to the ENGINE class as a gap-finder.** §11 refuses it with the
   distribution and names what to use instead.

**#200 IS NOT A FINISHED ISSUE, AND THE LIVE HALF IS THE ONE CLOSEST TO THE USER'S OWN WORDS.** The

## 5. My own mistakes, which are one mistake

**Eight of the ten were the same thing: I reasoned where I should have measured, and the first
number or claim was always TOO STRONG, never too weak.**

1. **I shouted inside a quotation of card text** in a staged entry — `'when I conquer AFTER AN
   ATTACK'` — **one hour after repairing that exact defect in the plays corpus.**
   `test/prose-emphasis.test.ts` caught it on the first full run. A guard written by somebody else,
   for a different corpus, beat my own recent memory of the rule.
2. **I read an inversion out of two corners of a table.** fury/chaos at the top of the engine column
   and the bottom of the finisher one, fury/calm the reverse — and Spearman rho over the fourteen
   pairs with four or more available finishers is **0.15**. Two corners are not a trend. Caught by
   computing the thing rather than looking at it.
3. **I told the manager an entry "cannot reach" states it reaches at two to four cards.** The Gutter
   Palace dials generate the whole lattice; the defect is COST, not reachability. Caught by writing a
   BFS while drafting the repair — after the report had gone.
4. **I claimed Chaos supplies the pure hand decrement.** `VEN-052 Mesmerize`'s second mode is (0,−1)
   and is **Mind**. Caught by building the dial table instead of reasoning about it.
5. **I ported the finisher spanning question to the ENGINE class unchanged**, and the manager had to
   tell me to expect a different KIND of answer. The distribution then proved it in one line: a
   spanning ENGINE has a median of two non-legend cards and a spanning finisher three.
6. **An alt-art sweep returned zero and I nearly wrote it down.** `corpus_flat.txt` is folded to base
   printings; `cards.json` has all 45. Caught by a second instrument, which is the standing rule.

7. **The emphasis guard caught me a THIRD time, in a staged entry, AFTER I had written mistake 1
   above into this very document.** `'Your CONQUER effects for conquering here trigger an additional
   time'` — shouted inside a quotation of card text again. Knowing the rule, having just repaired it
   twice, and having documented it, did not stop me doing it. **That is the strongest evidence this
   lane produced for cross-corpus guards: the guard is not a reminder, it is the only thing that
   works.**
8. **My own staging script was not idempotent and duplicated an entry.** `build-staged4.mjs` appends
   to the file it reads, so re-running it after fixing mistake 7 produced two copies of the same id.
   `CLAUDE.md` says *"a staging script APPENDS, never overwrites"*, and this is the other half of that
   rule: **an append-to-its-own-output script must be run exactly once, or be idempotent by id.** It
   was caught by the worktree validation (`dupes: 1`) before it reached the manager, which is what
   that validation is for — the entry count being 771 where it should have been 770 was the tell, and
   I nearly explained it away as the baseline moving.

9. **THE ONE WHERE I WAS RIGHT AND THE SHARED PRACTICE WAS NOT ENOUGH.** I said the manager's
   sixteen point cards was my seventeen minus `OGN-276` alone. They measured **their** sixteen: it
   holds `OGN-276` **and** `VEN-053` and is missing `OGN-290`. **Same total, different members — two
   sets called "the sixteen point cards".** Both diagnoses were correct about different populations,
   and **both of us had stated our predicate.** *State the predicate with the number* separates the
   populations; *name the members of any counted set* proves which one you meant. **A total can
   COLLIDE across two predicates, and then only the members disambiguate.** Neither of us listed our
   sixteen, and that one line would have caught it. The repair is to quote a **split** — *"17 match,
   14 gain"* cannot be double-subtracted; *"sixteen point cards"* was.

10. **I MEASURED THE RIGHT THING AND ANSWERED THE WRONG QUESTION, AND THE MANAGER REFUTED IT.** I
    claimed `scripts/adversarial-check.mjs`'s `baselineTurn` is a turn too fast for eight identities,
    because the pool's only two Energy-1 units are one Body and one Calm so only calm/body can field
    **both cards**. The measurement is correct. **The curve never needed two CARDS, it needed two
    BODIES**, and 103.2.b allows three copies of a name — two `UNL-111` cost 2 Energy and land on
    turn 1. The script's comment said *"plays two bodies on turn 1"*, precisely, **and I read "two
    bodies" as "two cards"**. Routing my fix would have put a real defect INTO the clock. **Right
    file, right measurement, wrong question — and the word I needed was already in the comment.**

**The one that generalises past this lane**: the manager's instruction to *measure the distribution
before writing the predicate* paid on its first outing and it did not pay in the way I expected. It
did not improve a predicate — **it found a defect nothing was looking for**, `role: "multiplier"` on
three `uses` rows, sitting in a cross-tab cell of size ONE that I had built to answer a completely
different question. The big cells were conventions. The defect was in the cell of one.
